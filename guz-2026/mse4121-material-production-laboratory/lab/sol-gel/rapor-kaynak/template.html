1) Aim

To synthesize SiO2 nanopowder by the sol-gel method, coat it by dip and spin coating, and examine the effect of SiO2 content on the contact angle and viscosity of PVA solutions.

2) Materials and Devices

TEOS, ethanol, distilled water, NH4F, NH4OH, PVA, Triton X-100; magnetic stirrer, balance, drying oven, furnace, laser, spin coater, FTO/ITO glass, Ostwald viscometer.

3) Experimental Procedure

  • Solution II (3 mL water + 5 mL ethanol) was added dropwise to Solution I (3 mL TEOS + 5 mL ethanol) under stirring; hydrolysis started (≡Si–OR + H2O → ≡Si–OH + ROH).
  • Stock catalyst (1.9 g NH4F + 23 mL NH4OH in 100 mL water [1]) was added 3 drops at a time until a coating formed on a dipped glass slide (12 drops in total).
  • Before gelation a glass slide was dip-coated. After gelation a laser was passed through water and the gel.
  • The gel was dried at 100 °C for 24 h and calcined at 500 °C for 1 h, and weighed after each step.
  • PVA suspensions with 0.5 g and 1 g SiO2 were spin-coated (3 drops, 500/800 rpm, 5/10 s). One drop of water was placed on each coating, and the viscosities were measured with the Ostwald viscometer (5 mL, water as reference).

4.2) Tyndall effect

The beam was invisible in water but visible in the gel, because the colloidal SiO2 particles scatter light. This shows that the gel is a colloid.

4.4) Contact angle and superhydrophobic surface

The contact angle decreased as SiO2 increased, so the surface became more hydrophilic, because silica carries polar Si–OH groups. To make it superhydrophobic, the Si–OH groups are replaced with a fluoroalkylsilane (FAS) [2]:

≡Si–OH + (C2H5O)3Si–CH2CH2(CF2)nCF3 → ≡Si–O–Si–CH2CH2(CF2)nCF3 + C2H5OH

The fluorinated groups lower the surface energy, and the SiO2 nanoparticles give roughness that traps air under the drop. Brassard et al. spin-coated FAS-treated silica nanoparticles in this way and obtained a contact angle of 151°, so rain drops roll off [2].

4.5) Spin coating: effect of rpm and time

Higher rpm → thinner film: the final thickness follows h ∝ ω−1/2 [3]; going from 500 to 800 rpm gives a ~20 % thinner film. Longer time → thinner only in the first seconds: after that, solvent evaporation stops the flow and the thickness stays constant [3]. This is why 5 s and 10 s gave the same coating.
